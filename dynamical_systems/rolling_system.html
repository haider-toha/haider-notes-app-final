Rolling Cylinder in a Free-Sliding Cradle

An original two‑degree‑of‑freedom, energy‑conserving multibody system

Generalized coordinates  q = (x, θ) ·  genuine rolling‑without‑slipping on a rigid body with real moment of inertia ·  no driving force, no damping

A uniform solid cylinder (mass m, radius r, moment of inertia I = ½m r2) rolls without slipping on the inner wall of a circular cradle of radius R. The cradle is rigidly mounted on a cart of mass M that is free to slide horizontally on a frictionless track. As the cylinder rocks back and forth inside the bowl, the cart recoils — the two motions are coupled purely through the rolling contact and gravity. Because the contact rolls (does no work) and nothing else dissipates, total mechanical energy is conserved, and because x is cyclic the horizontal momentum is conserved too.

Method. The model was derived from scratch with the Lagrangian recipe surveyed from the Learn Multibody Dynamics reference scripts (Moore, CC BY 4.0): assemble rigid‑body kinetic energy as translation of the mass centre plus ½Iω2, encode the rolling contact as a velocity (motion) constraint to eliminate the dependent spin coordinate, apply the Euler–Lagrange operator, reduce to first‑order state form, integrate numerically, and verify energy conservation by recomputing total mechanical energy from the state at every step. The system itself—a cylinder rolling inside a recoiling cradle—is an original construction, not reproduced from any worked example.
